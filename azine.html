01The summary

Feeds are built to keep you scrolling, not to help you find anything good. azine is a personal board of essays, movies, albums and novels, plus museum art, chosen for one person from a few sentences about what they love and don't, and from how they sort each day's picks. Every pick is a real work from a curated catalog, mostly from before 2021. No feeds, no influencers, no AI-written content.

  1. Describe your taste. The site opens on a wall of museum art, then a handwritten note asking what you love and don't, in 20 words or more. Claude reads it while you sign up.
  2. The board fills itself. Each work is described by its genres, subjects, tones and synopsis. Your likes are grouped into as many taste clusters as you really have, so a niche taste still gets its turn.
  3. Read, listen, watch. Today's picks, laid out as showcases, with a fresh wall of art from the world's museums.
  4. Sort. Drag each pick to "loved it" or "not for me", or mark it neutral. Every sort teaches the board what to bring next.
  5. It keeps going. Boxes renew on a cadence you set: essays daily, albums every three days, movies and novels weekly.

02The stack

ServerPython 3.13, FastAPI, uvicorn, SQLAlchemy, Alembic, APScheduler, scikit-learn
RecommenderTF-IDF embeddings and taste clusters (after Pinterest's PinnerSage)
AIThe Claude API reads taste answers and labels new items. It never writes what you read.
DataSQLite on a persistent disk, backed up daily
Front endServer-rendered Jinja pages, vanilla JavaScript, one stylesheet, no build step
HostingRender (Docker), deployed on every push; azine.io on Cloudflare
AppsAn installable web app, and an iOS app (Capacitor) that shows the live site
SourcesOMDb, Google Books, Open Library, iTunes Search, Substack and essay feeds, and museum collections (the Met, NGA, V&A, Wikimedia Commons)

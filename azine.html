The summary

01

Feeds are built to keep you scrolling, not to help you find anything good. azine is a curated catalog of essays, movies, albums and novels, made for enjoying the skill of essayists, film teams, novelists and musicians. Every work is real, drawn from lists of acclaimed work, mostly from before 2021 (plus festival films from 2022 on), and the catalog is the same for everyone. No accounts, no feeds, no influencers, no AI slop.

  1. Pick a category. E, M, A and N sit in the corners of the front page: essays, movies, albums and novels. Click one to open its catalog.
  2. Pick a time. A timeline narrows the catalog to when the work was made.
  3. Say what you want. Type it into "Vibes", like "I want to read about x". A Claude API call reads it and filters the catalog down to the works that really fit, best first. A wall of art from the Met fills the screen while it runs.
  4. Browse what's nearby. Each category is sorted into groups of similar work, named for what they share.

The stack

02
ServerPython 3.13, FastAPI, uvicorn, SQLAlchemy, Alembic, APScheduler, scikit-learn
SearchClaude Sonnet turns the prompt into a plan; TF-IDF embeddings gather candidates, each part of the prompt getting its share (after Pinterest's PinnerSage); Claude Haiku scores each one, and only a 75%+ fit comes back
Groupsk-means over the same embeddings, each group named from the labels it shares
AIThe Claude API filters the catalog by what you type and labels new works. It never writes what you read.
CatalogRolling Stone's 500 Greatest Albums and BBC Music Magazine's classical picks; films from 1940 to 2021, plus festival films (Cannes, Sundance, Toronto, Hong Kong); novels from 1850 on; essays from the Longform archive and the National Magazine Awards
DataSQLite on a persistent disk, backed up daily
Front endServer-rendered Jinja pages, vanilla JavaScript, one stylesheet, no build step
HostingRender (Docker), deployed on every push; azine.io on Cloudflare
AppsAn installable web app
SourcesOMDb, Google Books, Open Library, iTunes Search, Substack and essay feeds, the Library of Congress, and the Met's open-access art
